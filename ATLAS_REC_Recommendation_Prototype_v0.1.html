Simplified Preset

What is Customer Due Diligence (CDD)?

Think of CDD as "know exactly who you're doing business with." Before a bank opens an account, it must verify the customer's identity and understand how risky they are.

  • Verify identity — official documents (passport, NRIC) prove the person is real.
  • Assess risk — is this customer likely to be involved in money laundering?
  • Keep monitoring — CDD doesn't stop after onboarding; transactions are watched continuously.
💡 Why you missed Q3: Enhanced due diligence (EDD) kicks in only for high-risk customers — e.g. politically exposed persons — not for every new account.

Spotting Red Flags

A red flag is any signal that a transaction doesn't fit the customer's normal profile.

  • Transactions just below reporting thresholds (structuring).
  • Cross-border transfers to jurisdictions with weak AML controls.
  • Activity that suddenly doesn't match the customer's stated occupation or income.
💡 Why you missed Q9: the volume of a transfer alone isn't a red flag — it's the mismatch with the customer's profile plus the destination that matters.
